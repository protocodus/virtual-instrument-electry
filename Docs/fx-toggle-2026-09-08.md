# FX bypass and control spacing — 8 September 2026

The **FX OFF / FX ON** button in the Amplifier & FX header bypasses or enables
the complete pedal, amp/cabinet, compressor, delay and room chain. New instances
start with FX off. Click the button to hear the stored effects; bypassing again
retains their settings and leaves the dry guitar playing. Bypassed controls dim
and stop accepting edits. The toggle remains accessible by keyboard, and follows
host automation and restored state.

![Electry with FX off](screenshots/electry-standalone.png)

The editor is now 1180 × 904. The FX header has 28-pixel-high controls, a
12-pixel inset, 14 pixels between the toggle and Quality label, and 8 pixels
between that label and its selector. The amp buttons have a separate row and
6 pixels of clearance before the knobs. The added height preserves the other
control rows and the 100-pixel playing keyboard. The Output panel is wider,
giving Mono, Stereo and Double more horizontal padding and 6 pixels of
clearance before the master knob.

A 5 ms stereo crossfade handles live switching, including a reversal during
the fade. Stable bypass leaves the dry samples untouched and skips the FX
processor. The amplifier's acoustic feedback drive fades away through the
existing engine smoothing. The completed output still feeds the engine's
causal return path. Delay and room buffers clear once the fade reaches dry, so
re-enabling cannot replay a frozen tail.

The `fxEnabled` boolean is the 30th host parameter, appended at zero-based
index 29 with version hint 3. Earlier parameter indices stay intact. Its state
is saved with the patch. Existing sessions without this field migrate to FX
on, preserving their previous sound. Factory rig selection preserves the
current toggle state; enable FX to hear the amp in Drop-E Metal or Blues Rock
Lead.

## Validation

Native processor/editor checks cover the parameter contract, new-instance
default, saved on/off states, legacy migration, factory rig behavior, exact dry
bypass in Mono/Stereo/Double, signal-changing engagement, smooth transitions,
callback-size invariance and cleared effect tails. UI checks exercise host and
keyboard synchronization, disabled controls, focus navigation and control
spacing. The native VST3, AU and CLAP artifact tests check the updated host
parameter surface and saved state.

All four checks pass on macOS arm64, and VST3, AU, CLAP and Standalone build.
The dry comparison is sample-exact at 44.1, 96 and 192 kHz; live switching also
passes with 17-, 128- and 511-frame callbacks and 1024-frame callbacks after a
32-frame preparation. Normal, 2×, FX-on and sounding-performance captures were
visually reviewed. The existing realism DSP files match the completed pass 4.

Build and check using the existing native configuration:

```sh
cmake --build build-ui-20260908/native --target \
  ElectryPluginProcessorTests ElectryVST3ArtifactSmokeTests \
  ElectryAUArtifactSmokeTests ElectryCLAPArtifactSmokeTests Electry_Standalone \
  --parallel 6
ELECTRY_EDITOR_SNAPSHOT="$PWD/build-fx-toggle-20260908/final.png" \
  ctest --test-dir build-ui-20260908/native \
  -R 'PluginProcessor|VST3Artifact|CLAPArtifact|AUArtifact' --output-on-failure
```

Local logs and captures remain under `build-fx-toggle-20260908/`. These checks
measure audio signals and render the actual JUCE editor; they do not constitute
a listening evaluation.
