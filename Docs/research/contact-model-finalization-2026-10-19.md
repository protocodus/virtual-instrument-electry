## Why investigate continuous contact

The published [coherent-field comparison](coherent-field-tone-2026-10-19.md)
retains mixed outcomes against real dry guitar recordings. Its inherited-loss
field C improves all eight admitted 1.5–4 kHz slope errors, but fails every
eligible 4–8 kHz comparison and regresses several body measures at other fixed
controls. At Neck Pick .18, median H2/H1 and H3/H1 remain excessive by
+8.227 and +17.263 dB. At Pick .5, several harmonic and body errors worsen.
An improved broad bass band can conceal a weak fundamental and excessive
second or third harmonic; it cannot establish the desired lower-body balance.

The earlier [tone-body study](tone-body-2026-10-18.md) also distinguishes
initial spectrum from subsequent loss. A recording's pickup, string build,
pick force and stopping hand are not identified by its MIDI annotations.
These observations motivate separate excitation, observation and loss tests;
they do not justify fitting an output EQ or a universal damping correction.

The isolated fields used assumed initial storage and finite unloading.
They did not establish realistic same-note repicks, same-string note changes
or fret transitions. A playable field must retain one continuous string state
through those events, with each actuator's force and work accounted for.
Stitching independent note outputs would leave that requirement unanswered.

## Remaining state and contact ownership

There is no qualified invertible transfer from the production delay rings,
allpass/filter histories and refret banks into the proposed material field's
displacement and velocity coordinates. An audible old note cannot simply be
reinitialized when its logical fret changes. Mechanical contact arrival,
render scheduling and output histories also need a consistent clock.

A finger, a pick and a fret can act simultaneously. Permanent fret barriers,
moving finger geometry and release are distinct contact roles. A capacity-two
algebraic solver is insufficient to represent all such overlaps. Increasing
the numerical capacity alone does not supply the geometry, friction, physical
stiffness, contact motion or passive state update.

For moving contact geometry, actuator power must include the geometry-motion
term as well as normal support motion. A fixed contact gather cannot silently
stand in for a moving fret or finger. Finger/material parameters and a
physically admitted lifecycle remain separate qualification work.

## Capacity-five stored-equation prototype

The experimental C++17 kernel extends the stored binary64 contact equations
to at most five simultaneous scalar normal contacts. It retains every active
matrix term, an input-specific contraction bound, outward interval budgets
and the original finite `2e-11 N` residual rail. Its `K=6000` is a numerical
unit-fixture constant, not an identified finger or steel-fret stiffness.

The new sufficient iteration cap uses an outward geometric recurrence before
force iteration. Its administrative maximum of 4096 updates is a resource
ceiling; exhausting it without a certified bound is a rejection. A fixed
array layout and bounded loop do not establish a realtime execution budget.

## Event-relative contact service

The separate service draft expresses a contact recipe relative to its actual
event origin, with a finite press trajectory and pole-recurrence withdrawal.
It does not own the string state, energy ledger or a complete event queue.
Its source review is not an executed control result: the registered large
translation/withdrawal control study remains unexecuted.

Retirement is unresolved. A strong-to-soft event can leave positive support
height after the prescribed withdrawal. A quiet pickup output does not prove
zero stored contact energy, and instantaneous separation does not prove that
later recontact is impossible. Resetting an occupied contact, imposing a
timeout, or renormalizing its withdrawal would need new physical justification.

## Final source-study closure

This separate isolated-source study uses the earlier qualified contact kernel,
not the new capacity-five executable. Its outcome cannot qualify that new
implementation or the unexecuted event-relative service.

The once-only attempt closed **incomplete at 95 of 96 cases**. Its prospective
storage guard sent SIGINT when actual free space fell to 1,053,081,600 bytes,
below its fixed 1 GiB floor. The unfinished `bridge/sc050` case was in its
first reference history. Both model histories completed, but neither reference
history completed and no complete case result exists. The source exited
`-2`; the native transport closed cleanly. All partial evidence is retained.

The 95 completed primary cases selfreported PASS and all five rate rows PASS.
Those flags are not independent qualification. No complete aggregate exists.
The conditional handoff closed and correctly refused to run the independent
saved-data audit because the source was incomplete. The original 96-case
requirement was retained: no subset export, retry, shortened history or relaxed
physical gate was used.

## Qualification still required

Any production proposal needs independent complete mechanical replay before
candidate field/audio access; an owned continuous-state contact lifecycle;
work, dissipation, refinement and rate checks for overlapping repicks/refrets;
and explicit runtime, memory and alias qualification. Only then can complete
fixed-control phrases be compared against real dry recordings with retained
failures, consistent crops and whole-track matching. These studies establish
neither subjective naturalness nor 95% realism, and the available conventional
guitar references do not certify premium eight-string hardware fidelity.
