**Five further FX CPU options — measured September 8, 2026**

**Historical snapshot.** This report records the original prototype evaluation. The current build now includes the pedal inverse, PI scalar tables, exact identical-channel reuse, and Standard/High oversampling with its accepted rate/quality tradeoff. Integration estimates, memory counts and outstanding-validation notes below describe the earlier prototypes. See the [current implementation](../README.md#amplifier-chain), [integrated oversampling measurements](fx-cpu/selectable-oversampling/summary.csv), and [subsequent channel-reuse measurements](fx-cpu/mono-reuse/summary.csv) for the shipped result.

A further 20% reduction in effects CPU is achievable with very small measured audio differences. The strongest general candidate combines a prepared pedal inverse with faster phase-inverter scalar math: complete chains used **38.0% less CPU for American, 38.6% for British and 42.8% for Modern** on actual ElectryEngine guitar input. Synthetic stereo probes gave **41.4%, 41.4% and 61.5%**, respectively. Signal dependence matters; the guitar results are the better practical headline.

These results are relative to the already optimized `f2a5258b7f14b5dc72ef87250636f3021b3dc241`, not the earlier pre-optimization revision. The candidates are retained in [Experiments/FxCpuOptions](../Experiments/FxCpuOptions/README.md). This evaluation does not enable them in the production build.

The percentages in this table use the repeated synthetic-input campaign. Individual amp cases have no pedal; complete chains enable pedal, amp, compressor, delay and room. Tiny changes on unaffected paths, generally around ±2%, are timing noise. Savings from independent options must not be added; the combined candidate was measured separately.

For option 1, the original maximum current residual is `1e-10` A. Both `1e-8` and `1e-7` were tested without changing the 12-iteration ceiling or state-commit safeguards. Forty circuit probes span two amps, five internal rates, four drive levels and post-burst recovery; none failed the commit guard. Earlier convergence still perturbs the stored capacitor/grid state. The conservative setting passed both audio corpora, while the aggressive setting's automation result argues against using it for this task. The code change is one constant; estimate half to one working day for focused integration and validation.

For option 2, the pedal's monotonic trapezoidal Shockley RC equation is inverted in `prepare()`. A 4,097-point positive-domain table uses cubic Hermite interpolation and odd symmetry, retaining a direct solver outside its bounds. The new path solves the same implicit equation and showed lower worst equation residual than the reference's four-iteration solver in the measured probes; its finite differences from the reference are not evidence of a different intended transfer curve. Dense probes at 64–768 kHz internal rates were finite and monotonic, with maximum equation residual at most `3.31e-11` (at most `8.81e-13` at the production 352.8/384 kHz rates). The table takes **65,552 bytes per instance** and **0.69–0.74 ms median** to construct. Construction and allocation happen outside audio processing. Before shipping, retain direct tests of the new production path, its bounds/fallback, repeated preparation and capacitor-state recovery; existing inspection tests still exercise the reference diode solver.

For option 3, prepared scalar tables approximate softplus and the tube power functions, including derivatives consistent with the actual interpolation polynomial. Exact library math remains the fallback outside the table domain; idle calibration and direct curve inspection remain exact. Maximum measured scalar softplus error was `3.10e-10`, with power-function relative error at most `4.75e-12`. The tables occupy **59,936 shared bytes**, initialized before processing. This avoids repeated expensive scalar functions inside the unchanged coupled Newton solve. The full existing FX suite passed, including circuit anchors, residuals, recovery, alias boundaries, model transitions and block invariance. Retain the scalar accuracy probes and test supported release platforms before promotion.

For option 4, one oversampling stage is removed after the existing stage selector: 44.1/48 kHz goes from 8× to 4× and 96 kHz from 4× to 2×. At 48 kHz, full-drive Modern alias energy rises from **−72.70 to −61.28 dB** relative to harmonics; American rises from **−70.82 to −62.15 dB**. The existing limit is −70 dB, and baseline passes all 18 probes. Pedal alone passes at all three rates, so a selective pedal-rate design could warrant a separate experiment, but this global rate cut cannot support an inaudibility claim. Gain-path latency decreases by 2.875 host samples at 44.1/48 kHz and 5.75 at 96 kHz, about 60–65 microseconds. After fractional alignment, individual American/pedal/Modern steady-signal RMS differences at 48 kHz remain around −57 to −62 dBFS. Raw, unaligned nulls exaggerate the change and are retained only for transparency. Keeping quality at the lower rate would require additional nonlinear antialiasing work and fresh measurement; its cost and achievable saving are not established by this prototype.

For option 5, only the nonlinear gain chain is shared. Delay and room retain their independent stereo histories. On the first divergent input sample, the right gain state is synchronized from the left before either channel renders; subsequently distinct histories stay separate until a full gain reset. Merely receiving equal samples later does not make the histories equal. Thus the saving applies to sustained matching input/history, with no benefit promised for Stereo/Double modes. The default first-divergence copy is 3,152 bytes; the optional measured cabinet adds 146,452 bytes. That nondefault build's transition timing and quality still need dedicated coverage. Estimate one to two working days for production lifecycle and transition validation.

The recommended next implementation combines options 2 and 3. It preserves oversampling and algorithmic latency, adds no audio-thread allocation, and passed the full existing FX suite. The synthetic quality corpus covers **9,028,800 float samples** across 12 scenarios and 44.1/48/96 kHz; the four-second guitar corpus adds **4,608,000 samples** across 12 scenarios at 48 kHz. Combined worst errors were:

| Corpus | Peak absolute difference | RMS difference, worst scenario |
|---|---:|---:|
| Synthetic stereo, including automation and release tails | `9.1344e-6` FS | **−125.62 dBFS** |
| Actual modeled guitar, including low notes, chords, varied velocity and muting | `4.6119e-6` FS | **−134.40 dBFS** |

These differences support the requested tiny, inaudible-change target on the tested material. No controlled listening test was performed, and short deterministic probes do not establish behavior for every possible preset or performance. The pre-rendered guitar fixture also excludes the plugin's live amp-to-string acoustic-return loop; promotion should include resonance/feedback presets in the actual plugin. The numerical acceptance budget was peak `5e-5` FS and RMS `1e-6` FS (−120 dBFS), used as a regression criterion rather than a universal audibility threshold.

For the combination, added table storage is **125,488 bytes for the first instance**, then 65,552 bytes per additional instance because the PI tables are shared, plus small bookkeeping/alignment overhead. Plan **one to two working days** for integration and focused validation on this Mac, or **two to four days** including available macOS/Windows release checks and listening review. These are engineering estimates. The main work is retained production-path regression coverage, lifecycle/fallback tests and release-platform measurements; the measured prototype already demonstrates the CPU target. Combining identical-channel reuse with these tables is an additional opportunity, but that three-way combination has not been benchmarked.

Measurements used an Apple M1 Max, macOS 26.5.1, Apple Clang 21, C++20 `-O3 -DNDEBUG`, default cabinet configuration and 128-frame blocks. Primary CPU data used four alternating candidate sweeps, each bracketed by baseline runs, with five repeats of 0.5 seconds per scenario. The reported saving is the median of each sweep's candidate/baseline ratio. Combined complete-chain savings ranged **40.1–43.0% American, 39.6–43.3% British and 60.7–62.8% Modern** across those sweeps. At 96 kHz a shorter supplementary sweep measured **42.2%, 42.0% and 61.9%**.

The guitar timing check used baseline/combined/combined/baseline order, two seconds per scenario and three repeats. Its absolute median process CPU time changed from **10,680.7 to 6,621.4 ns/stereo frame** for American, **10,378.6 to 6,376.7** for British and **2,509.1 to 1,435.4** for Modern. Guitar synthesis was outside the timer. All timing was serial, with no concurrent compilation or audio tests. `std::clock` process time is primary, with `steady_clock` elapsed time as corroboration. Input generation/copying, preparation, table warmup, reset and checksums are excluded; effect processing and per-block parameter updates are included. CPU time excludes descheduling, but frequency, core migration and thermal/cache effects still introduce uncertainty.

Identical-channel reuse timing used a separate baseline/candidate/candidate/baseline sequence at 48 kHz, 0.5 seconds per scenario and five repeats, with exactly equal L/R input. Each timed repeat starts from bypass and a reset state, so these figures average engagement and the specified clip rather than measuring settled-only throughput. Oversampling-aligned comparisons use an FFT fractional shift on the two-second captures and assess the steady 0.5–1.45-second region to avoid startup and tail edges.

These are **effects CPU savings**, not whole-plugin savings. If effects consume half the instrument's CPU, a 40% FX reduction would reduce total CPU by about 20%; the effects share of the whole plugin was not measured here.
